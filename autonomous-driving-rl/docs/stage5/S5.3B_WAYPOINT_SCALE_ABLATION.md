# S5.3B: fixed waypoint-scale ablation

This is a separate development experiment motivated by the S5.3A activation
diagnostic. It does not replace or rewrite S5.3A, alter RewardSpecV1, or confer
benchmark eligibility.

## Hypothesis

The S5.2 policy's first ReLU layer became almost entirely inactive during the
10k-step pilot. A plausible contributor is the magnitude mismatch between
bounded core features and raw route waypoint coordinates. This ablation tests
whether reducing only waypoint relative-coordinate magnitudes changes actor
activation and deterministic driving behavior.

## Single changed factor

After the unchanged Platform V1 `Stage5ObservationEncoder` emits its 409D
vector, divide each of the 20 waypoint `rel_x` and `rel_y` features by a fixed
50 metres. The segment is `[325:385]`, arranged as 20 rows of
`[rel_x, rel_y, rel_heading]`. Heading, masks, core, traffic and task scalars
are unchanged. No statistics are fitted from pilot/diagnostic data and no
clipping is applied. SAC, reward, seed, geometry split, environment seeds,
episode horizon and checkpoint schedule remain the pilot settings.

The 50m divisor is an explicit scale choice based on observed waypoint range;
it is not claimed to be an optimal or universal physical normalization. This
is a diagnostic contrast, not a benchmark result.

## Provenance and compatibility

The transform has ID `pilot_waypoint_xy_div50m_v1`; its canonical specification
SHA-256 is recorded in each checkpoint. The frozen encoder schema fingerprint
is retained separately, and `policy_input_fingerprint` binds encoder plus
transform. Historical checkpoints with no transform continue to use raw
observations. The transform is applied only when a checkpoint explicitly
declares the supported transform.

The internal 156/24 TRAIN split and 24-case diagnostic schedule remain as in
S5.3A. Diagnostic geometries remain excluded from training and no official
VALIDATION/TEST case is used. Results must be compared by paired case against
the corrected raw-input run, with failed/incomplete episodes reported as such.
